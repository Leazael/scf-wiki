Before her [[Tiamat#Disappearance|disappearance]], Tiamat, or more accurately _Thjamád_, was a colossal black [[Dragon]] that ruled [[Nachýr]]  as both its [[God|deity]] and its empress. 

Her rule can reliably traced back to earliest days following the [[Rupture of the Æther]] and the [[Foreworld]]. This made her one of the oldest known beings in history.

She was known for being a harsh ruler who imposed a strict social hierarchy, with [[Serpentfolk]] at the top, followed by [[Kovold|Kobolds]], and with [[Lizardfolk]] and [[Human|Humans]] and other [[Sapient]] slaves at the very bottom. 

Tiamat was, and by some still is, revered as a [[God]] by the citizens of [[Nachýr]]. Obeisance was typically paid in the form of sacrifices of food, jewlery, precious items and slaves, which were funnelled by the priestly classes to Taimat's palace in [[Chýrusandr]].

# Mythology

Tiamat plays a central role in the [[Nachýrian Religion|religion]] of [[Nachýr]]. She is generally regarded as the egg-sister of the great dead dragon [[Vahamut]], with whom she has always shared a strong rivalry. As such, the veneration of [[Vahamut]] was forbidden to the citizens and slaves of [[Nachýr]].

Within the [[Crucible]] and in particular within the[[Church of Iomedæ]], there is still some discussion related to the divinity of Tiamat. Some belief that the empress Tiamat was indeed a [[God|deity]] physically presiding in [[Chýrusandr]]. Others hold that Tiamat-the-Empress was simply an "aspect" or "avatar" of a greater being.

# Appearance

[[Crucible]] eyewitnesses have described her as a an immensely large black dragon, with some accounts estimating her full length to be close to 50 [[Philosophical Units|meters]], and a similar wingspan. Her head alone is said to be the size two elephants standing abreast, with teeth the size of a grown man's forearm. Her forward facing black horns are said to be longer than three men laid lenthwise. 

In contrast to the classic [[Dragon|draconic]] description of [[Crucible]] scholars, [[Nachýrian Religion]] generally depicts her as having five heads of differing colours.

# History

Tiamat's presence in history is significant, dating from the founding of the [[Crucible]] and possibly the formation of [[Urth]], until her recent [[Tiamat#Disappearance|disappearance]]. 

Her presence in the [[Crucible]] was first felt in the 6th century, as the [[Nachýr|Nachýrian Empire]] expanded its borders eastwards. This expansion led to conflict with [[Izmoroz]], resulting in the [[War of Frozen Chains ]]from [[566 AF]] to [[581 AF]]. In the aftermath, Tiamat moved her capital eastwards to the newly renamed city of [[Chýrusandr]].

In the centuries following the War, increased contact between the [[Crucible]] and [[Nachýr]] caused the worship of Tiamat and its practice of slavery to slowly spread to neighbouring countries. This provoked the [[Church of Iomedæ]] in [[Arcadia]], leading to the launch of the First [[Crusade]] in [[822 AF]]. 

This conflict lasted for centuries, with the [[Crusade|Counter Crusade]] being particularly infamous. During this time, Tiamat herself flew to the [[Crucible]] to lay waste to [[Arcadia]]'s allies, resulting in the [[Ilm's Watch|Sack of Ilm's Watch]], during which the largest city of the [[Crucible]] was levelled by Tiamat's onslaught.

Peaceful relations with the [[Crucible]] were not re-established until the 17th century. Notably, in [[1691 AF]], Lethania purchased its first [[Lethanian Colonies|colony]] from Tiamat herself.

# Disappearance 

Throughout the 1760s, it slowly became clear to the countries of the [[Crucible]] that a catastrophe was taking place in [[Nachýr]]. A deadly disease known as the [[Withering Blossoms]] was spreading rapidly among the [[Serpentfolk]], leaving none alive. During this time, all formal communications between [[Nachýr]] and the [[Crucible]] were halted. 

In [[1768 AF]] the silence was broken by the start of the [[Nachýrian Exodus]], which brought with it the news that Tiamat had disappeared, and that [[Vahamut|Vahamut's Corpse]] had been discovered.

The chaos caused by the collapse of [[Nachýr]]'s governing class has made it all but impossible to retrace what exactly transpired, and Tiamat's disappearance remains an enigma to this day.
