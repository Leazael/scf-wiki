Revelationary or Apocalyptic politics are the range of ideals and ideologies that developed around the [[Revelationary Revolution]]. Its core principles encode a demand for the dissemination of the various trade-secrets, mysteries and other obscured knowledge employed by the upper classes of societies. 

The target of these demands is and was mainly directed towards the aristocracy, but also various, guilds, universities and religious institutions. 

As the movement has never been centralized, differing apocalyptic movements have drafted different programs. Common items on these programs include:
- The abolishment of intellectual property.
- The dissemination of trade secrets held by guilds and artisans and the [[Great Guilds of Ilmbund|Great Guilds]] in particular.
- The dissemination of [[Alchemy|Alchemical]] processes employed by various industries.
- The dissemination of all state secrets.
- Free and open publication of all [[University|academic]] research.
- Freedom of dissemination and the cessation of state censorship.
- Freedom of association and assembly.
- Full suffrage regardless of class, and other democratic reforms. 
- The abolishment of all secret societies. 
- Access to secular (i.e., not [[Church of Iomedæ|church-controlled]]) healthcare.
- Public negotiations between employers and employees, by means of organized trade unions and guilds.

Many urban apocalyptic movements have at their core also been labour-movements. As such, rights for unions, shorter workweeks, and other pro-labour demands have traditionally been part of many apocalyptic programs. 

Pro-labour and anti-capital revelationary politics have become a core of the many [[Merovianism|Merovian]] movements that have sprung up around the [[Crucible]] in the last two decades.

[[Lethania|South-Lethania]], [[Ilmbund]] and to a lesser degree [[Arcadia]] have instituted many social and democratic reforms in the spirit of apocalyptic politics in the wake of the [[Revelationary Revolution]].

# Criticisms
Those who oppose revelationary politics often cite the following criticisms:
- Intellectual property is necessary to foster innovation.
- State secrets may be necessary for national defence. 
- Censorship may be needed to protect the people from harmful or false information.
- Not all people are capable or sufficiently informed to participate in voting.

Many have also observed that the prohibition of trade and industrial sequence has led to a degree of secrecy by obfuscation. That is, companies and institutions regularly report incomplete and erroneous data alongside real information, thus increasing the cost for competitors by forcing them to weed out real information from the fake.
