The Three Lays form the first part of the [[Iomedæan Canon]] of the [[Church of Iomedæ]]. The Three Lays are long narrative poems that detail the birth and death of [[Iomedæ]] and the founding of the [[Church of Iomedæ]]. Parts of the Lays are often sung during mass and festivals.

The Lays themselves were supposedly written in the years after her apotheosis. It is said that when Iomedæ heard the Three Lays for the first time, she shed three tears; an event that is itself described in the [[Three Lays|Lay of Stone]].

The oldest versions of these books are roughly 1900 years old, and hail from [[Vahestan]].

# The Lay of Steel
The first Lay narrates the foretelling and the birth of [[Iomedæ]], and her early life as a shield-maiden.

# The Lay of Blood 
The second Lay tells of a great [[Rupture of the Æther|cataclysm]] that tore the world asunder. It then deals with [[Iomedæ]]'s rejection of the existing [[God|gods]], and her assault on the heavens, backed by her [[Twelve Paladins]]. At the start of the assault, she discards her [[Scabbard of Iomedæ|Scabbard]], vowing to slay all [[God|gods]] who oppose her. Having conquered the heavens though, she finds herself still filled with righteous wrath, symbolised by her unsheathed sword. To shield the innocents from her wrath, she sheathes her sword in her own blood and flesh in her final act of mercy. This sacrifice is what results in her final apotheosis.

# Lay of Stone 
The third and final Lay tells of the founding of the [[Church of Iomedæ]] and the establishment of her priesthood. It is told from the viewpoint of the [[Twelve Paladins]].

Throughout the [[Three Lays|Lay of Stone]] she appears in increasingly divine apparitions to the surviving [[Twelve Paladins|Paladins]]. Here she guides the greater [[Saint|Saints]] in the founding of the [[Church of Iomedæ|Church]]. She also hands down the [[Six Tenets]], relates the [[Nine Parables]] and listens to the [[Three Lays]], which move her to tears.
