The Laws of fumigation are a collection of mathematical expressions that govern the behaviour of fumes and fumigations. 

These laws have been developed by a collection of [[Natural Philosophy|Natural Philosophers]] and [[Alchemy|Alchemists]] over the last centuries, and have more recently led to the discovery of the [[Caloric Æther]].

# Cardinal Law of Fumigation
The Cardinal Law of Fumigation posits a relation between the pressure $P$, mass-density $\rho$ and temperature $T$ of a fume:
$$
P = \lambda \rho c T
$$
where the fumigative proportion constant $\lambda$ is a dimensionless constant specific to the fumigation, $\rho$ is the density of the fumigation, and $c$ is the specific [[Caloric Æther|Caloric]] capacity (at constant volume) of the fumigation.

# Rate of Transmutation

The rate of a transmutation $Z$ scales with specific transmutation-temperature $\tau$ and the temperature as:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Z &\propto T^{\alpha} \exp \left(-\frac{\tau}{T} \right) \prod_i \rho_i^{\sigma_i}
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\rho_i$ are the densities of the reagents, and $\sigma_i$ is their stoichiometric multiplicity. 

Note that the transmutation-temperature can be negative, in the case of a spontaneous transmutation.

The exponential factor $\alpha$, is generally positive but smaller than one. Most models predict either $\alpha = 0, \frac12$ or $1$.
