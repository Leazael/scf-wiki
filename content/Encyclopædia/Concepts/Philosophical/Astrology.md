Astrology is the branch of [[Natural Philosophy]] that studies the motions of the [[Star|Stars]] and [[Planet|Planets]], as well as the study of [[Calendar|time and dating]] in general.

Below, some common astrological notions are expanded upon.

# Nychthemeron
A Nychthemeron, or more colloquially "day" is the period from sunset to sunset, subdivided into 24 hours. 

As the movement of the [[Sun]] around [[Urth]] is slightly erratic due to many small retrograde and prograde movements, a consistent notion of how long a "day" exactly lasts is hard to measure directly. 

Instead, a day is formally defined to be exactly 413 / 148665 part of a (sidereal) year, meaning that a year lasts approximately 359.96 days. The year can be more precisely measured than the day, however, parallax effects still make a direct measurement tricky. 

As the rotation of [[Urth]] aligns with the rotation of the [[Sun]], a sidereal day is just under four minutes shorter than a solar day. Hence, affixed to the [[Star|stars]], a year lasts approximately 361 sidereal days instead. A full year contains exactly 149078 / 413 sidereal days, i.e., one more than the number of solar days.

# [[Calendar|Leap Year]]
Most years are Common Years of 360 days, however, every 28th year, starting from [[28 AF]], is a Leap Year, which consists of only 359 days. 

According to some of the few [[Astrology|Philosophers]] that track the motions of the [[Star|Stars]] and [[Planet|Planets]], an extra day should be skipped every 1652 years. This, however, is not observed by any known calendar, as the effect is rather minor.

# Planetary Orbits
The motion of the planets is governed by the numbers 2, 3, 5, 11, 17, 53 and 73. Due to the precise interplay of these numbers, the planets align themselves into a straight line every 2482 years. The last such occurrence was on the last day of the Grassmonth in [[1766 AF]].

During this time, the firmament itself rotates (720/13)×(2 - ϕ) degrees w.r.t. the [[Luminiferous Æther]].

The orbits of the planets are not perfectly planar but vary by small amounts (between -3 and 3 degrees). However, the orbit of the [[Sun]] is significantly more tilted on an angle of 18 degrees with respect to the [[Luminiferous Æther]], which causes the procession of the [[Calendar|Seasons]]. This also means that any Alignment (full or partial) always coincides with the equinoxes. 

# Epochs
The year [[0 AR]] started started on the Vernal Equinox (as seen from the northern hemisphere of [[Urth]]). After 60/17 hours, it was midday in [[Ilm's Watch]].

The year [[0 AF]] ended and the year [[1 AF]] started on the winter solstice, nine months after the vernal equinox marking the start of [[0 AF|716 AR]].

More precisely, astrologers generally date the beginning of [[1 AF]] at midnight to the moment 4386062/17 days since the first alignment.
