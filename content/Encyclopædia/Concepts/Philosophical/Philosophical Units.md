The Philosophical Units are the units most commonly used by [[Natural Philosophy|Natural Philosophers]]. These units include those derived from the Bundesmeter, the Bundeskilogram and the second. 

The former two are so named in reference to artefacts kept by the Bundes-Office of Standards and Measurements in [[Sälen]], [[Ilmbund]]. Before their dissolution, these artefacts were instead maintained by the [[Guild of Stonemasons]] and were recovered in the wake of the [[Twelve-Day-War]].

Most of these units are often modified by prefixes such as milli- \[m\], kilo- \[k\], mega- \[M\], etc. The prefix b-, denoting a reference to the standardised Bundesmeter or Bundeskilogram, is generally only used in the absence of other prefixes.

# Second
Time is usually measured in seconds \[s\]. The second is defined with respect to the length of a full [[Astrology|Sidereal Year]]. In particular:
$$
1 \text{ second} = \frac{413}{12844656000} \text{Year}.
$$

# Distance 
Length is usually measured in meters \[bm\]. The meter was originally defined as one-fifth of the greatest common denominator of the dimensions of the [[Walls of Elbisborg]]. This definition has since been replaced by a reference to a physical reference meter stored at the Bundes-Office of Standards and Measurements in [[Sälen]].

# Volume
The Bundesliter \[bl\] is so named because it is derived from the meter. It is in fact defined by 
$$
1 \text{ Bl} = 10^{-3} \times \text{Bm}^3
$$

# Mass 
The Bundeskilogram \[bkg\] was originally defined as the mass of a litre of water. However, also this unit has since been replaced by an artefact held at the Bundes-Office.

# Other units

## Force
Force is sometimes expressed in terms of mass, but more rigorous [[Natural Philosophy|Philosophical]] operations use the neustadt \[N\]. The neustadt is defined as 
$$
\text{N} = \text{bkg} \frac{\text{bm}}{\text{s}^2}
$$

## Pressure 
Pressure is most often measured in millimetres of [[Quicksilver]] denoted as $☿{\text{mm}}$. Alternatively, pressure is also expressed in terms of neustadt per meter squared as
$$
1 \text{ }☿{\text{mm}} = \frac{400}{3} \text{ N} \cdot \text{bm}^{-2}.
$$

Quicksilver has been historically used to measure air-pressure. As such, the standard value for air pressure at sea level has been historically defined as 
$$
P = 760 \text{ }☿{\text{mm}} = 760 \times \frac{400}{3}\text{ N} \cdot \text{bm}^{-2}.
$$

## Energy 
While there have been many historic units for energy, the prevailing unit is the jäger \[J\] (lowercase when spelt out, upper case in abbreviation), named for the Alchemist [[Æmilia Jäger]], the inventor of the [[Miasma]]-wick. The jäger is defined as 
$$
\text{ J} = \text{bkg} \frac{\text{bm}^2}{\text{s}^2}
$$

## Electrical Units
As [[Electricity|Electrical]] phenomena have received more [[Natural Philosophy|Philosophical]] scrutiny, several electrical units have arisen.

- The conund \[C\] for static electrical charges.
- The ambarical \[A\] for dynamic electrical charges.
- The vinah \[B\] for dynamic potential.

## Temperature
Temperature is usually measured in degrees centigrade \[°C\], or if absolute temperatures are required, in kälte, \[K\].

Temperature is generally defined utilizing the freezing and boiling points of water, with 0 °C corresponding to freezing and 100 °C being the boiling point.

## [[Hermetic Philosophy|Hermetic]] Units

Several units have been proposed in attempts to quantify [[Hermetic Philosophy|Hermetic]] dimensions, like Sympathy and Correspondence. However, no units have been accepted by the Bundes-Office as of yet.
