Mikhail Pjotr Lev Valdemar Merovitz (different publications use different first names) was a mysterious yet prolific [[Revelationary Politics|apocalyptic]] thinker, [[Wirtship|wirtships]]-[[Natural Philosophy|philosopher]], author and polemist who laid the groundwork for the branch of [[Revelationary Politics|apocalyptic politics]] now known as [[Merovianism]].

Very little is known of his life, though popular culture presents him as being born in the early 1710s in the eastern parts of [[Las]], now part of [[Polovina]]. He presumably attended the [[Academy of Weißenfels]] during the early days of the [[Revelationary Revolution]].

He was mostly active in the late 1740s and throughout the 1750s, in the aftermath of the [[Lethanian War of Separation]] and the [[Silent War]]. His most well-known works deal with justice, [[Wirtship|wirtship]], civics, and the nature of [[Sapient|sapient]] society.

He is frequently depicted as a middle-aged [[Human|human]] man, with long white hair, a long rough beard and a fierce frown.

# Controversy
The most pervasive controversy surrounding the life and authorship of Merovitz is related to the extent of his bibliography. 

It is quite accepted amongst both historians and [[Merovianism|Merovians]] that a number of the articles published under his name are not by his hand, but by other [[Revelationary Politics|apocalyptic]] thinkers using his name as a pseudonym. To this day, there is even among [[Merovianism|Merovians]] no consensus on exactly which articles are by him, and which are not. 

While the [[Merovianism|Merovian]] mainstream has generally accepted the importance (if not the authorship) of all supposedly Merovian documents, there exist factions who have embraced only a subset of the [[Merovianism|Merovian]] canon. Apocryphally, there even exists a faction of "Antimerovians", who embrace all [[Merovianism|Merovian]] works _except_ those written by him. 

The controversy is further heightened by the lack of historical records pertaining to his life, often attributed to the destruction of documents during the [[Lethanian War of Separation|Great War]]. Not even his death is agreed on, as to date there are seven different graves which supposedly hold his body. Others hold that Merovitz is still alive, and lives in exile somewhere in [[Izmoroz]].

Some have even speculated that he did not exist at all and that the name Merovitz is just a collective pseudonym used by a collective of [[Revelationary Politics|apocalyptic]] authors. This stance is, however, unilaterally disavowed by all [[Merovianism|Merovians]].
