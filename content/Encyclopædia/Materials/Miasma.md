Miasma is a heavy luminescent gas that can be found beneath the bogs and mires of the [[Hèrlmersc]] and the [[Myrk]] and is primarily used as a highly efficient energy source in many [[Natural Philosophy|philosophical]] and [[Alchemy|Alchemical]] processes. More mundanely, miasma has also become popular as a fuel for highly compact and efficient new lamps and other appliances.

Miasma can be easily recognized by its vibrant colours, alternating randomly between vibrant swirls of turquoise and fuchsia. Although miasma can be seen in the dark, it typically does not provide much light. A 50Bml vial of miasma sheds significantly less light than a typical candle.

Ever since the invention of the first Miasma-wicks in [[1634 AF]] by the [[Alchemy|Alchemist]] [[Æmilia Jäger]], other [[Natural Philosophy|Philosophers]] have found a myriad of applications for miasma. Most notably, the substance can be used to apply a great amount of heat in a very short amount of time, thus enabling engineers to start up their steam-engines in a fraction of the time it would take conventional fuels.

It has also been used as a propellant in several more exotic siege-weapons and as a power source for many other quaint and curious devices. In recent years, miasma has become commonly available with more and more houses and even street-lights being lit by the substance. Miasma lamps shed a harsh white-bluish light, that tends to flicker pinkly as the energy of the vial is depleted. A common street-lamp can be powered for well over a week by a single vial of miasma.

Although useful, miasma is also highly volatile and reacts explosively to sudden changes in temperature and pressure as well as impacts. The vials in which miasma is stored usually protect against the smallest shocks. Dropping such a vial from head-height may not lead to an explosion, but any impact greater will probably cause the miasma to detonate.

Most of the available miasma is won directly from the marshes in the [[Hèrlmersc]] in [[Lethania]] and the [[Myrk]] in [[Arcadia]], though some deposits have also been found on several of the islands in the Shimmering Straits as well as swampy areas in [[Nachýr]]. Rumours are told that deposits of "solid miasma" have also been found deep beneath the mountains of [[Dvårgheim]].

The [[LoeterCo]] company has a majority stake in nearly all miasma-delving enterprises in the [[Crucible]].

# Properties
Miasma is a [[Four Elements#Deuterean Elements|Deuterean Gas]] with a [[Four Elements#Protohydric Index|Protohydric Index]] of 0. 

At room temperature, and under typical atmospheric pressure, Miasma has a density of 19.10 [[Philosophical Units|bg/bl]], which is an order of magnitude denser than that of air, and significantly heavier than any other known gas. 

A standard 50 [[Philosophical Units|ml]] vial of miasma contains 8.923 [[Philosophical Units|MJ]] of energy. This is roughly equal to 2 [[Philosophical Units|bkg]] of TNT. When correctly applied, one standard 50 [[Philosophical Units|ml]] vial of miasma can bring more than 25 [[Philosophical Units|bl]] of water from room-temperature to boiling within a minute. For larger [[Electricity|electric appliances]], larger commercial tank sizes are available.

One standard 50 [[Philosophical Units|ml]] vial of miasma at room temperature (20°C) contains 0.646 [[Philosophical Units|bg]] of Miasma.

All Miasma is generally stored an under-pressure of 515 [[Philosophical Units|☿mm]] (i.e., 255 [[Philosophical Units|☿mm]] below the standard pressure of 760 [[Philosophical Units|☿mm]]). 

Miasma has an energy density of 13.81 [[Philosophical Units|MJ/bg]]. This corresponds to an energy density of 263.4 [[Philosophical Units|MJ/bl]] at standard room temperature and atmospheric pressure, or 178.5 [[Philosophical Units| MJ/bl]] at vial-pressure. 
 
When depleted, miasma takes on the form of a light, inert dust. Depleted miasma is strongly hydro- and lipophobic, but highly soluble in [[Quicksilver]]. When such a [[Quicksilver]] solution is properly aerosolized and brought into contact with metastable miasma in the presence of a proper anode and cathode, an [[Alchemy|Alchemical]] reaction occurs, known as the [[Holtzmann-Effect]]. This reaction rapidly depletes the surrounding miasma but efficiently releases a large amount of electricity in the process.

In addition to its volatility, miasma has many poorly understood psychoactive properties. Breathing in the miasma may lead to mood-swings, paranoia and ultimately madness. The longer the exposure, the more violent and permanent the symptoms. 

Miasma has also been found usual in a number of [[Magic|Magical]] applications, and the application of miasma-powered engines to [[Hermetic Philosophy]] is currently being studied at the [[College of Elbisborg]].

# Critical Conditions
The critical pressure, above which miasma will spontaneously, and violently, discharge or decay depends on the temperature according to:

$$ 
P^☉(T) = P^🜍 \sqrt{ \frac{T}{T^🜍} \exp\left(\frac{T^🜍}{T}-1\right) },
$$

where $T^🜍 = 154.22 \text{°C}$ and where $P^🜍 = P^☉(T^🜍) = 756.18\ ☿\text{mm}$ is the lowest possible pressure at which miasma decay can occur.

Under normal atmospheric pressure of $760\ ☿\text{mm}$, [[Miasma]] becomes critical (i.e., decays or discharges) when exposed to temperatures in excess of $98.87 \text{°C}$. 

Under storage conditions in a properly pressurized vial, miasma only decays when exposed to temperatures in excess of $157.29 \text{°C}$.

If a vial were to be filled at a standard pressure of $760\ ☿\text{mm}$ and room temperature (i.e., $20 \text{°C}$), such a vial would explode if exposed to temperatures above $28.64 \text{°C}$.

It is well understood that [[Miasma]] rarely discharges due to temperature, in fact at standard atmospheric pressure, [[Miasma]] only discharges between temperatures of $98.87 \text{°C}$ and $221.13 \text{°C}$. 

Instead, [[Miasma]] is very sensitive to pressure. It is not a matter of a spark igniting the miasma, instead at some distance close to a spark, a small amount of [[Miasma]] will go critical. The resulting increase in temperature then causes the increase in pressure needed to make neighbouring [[Miasma]] go critical. The resulting pressure wave is what then causes all surrounding [[Miasma]] to go critical.
