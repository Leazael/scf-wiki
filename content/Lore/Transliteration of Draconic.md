There are several known transcriptions schemes used for transcribing [[Draconic]] using the [[Ildarin]] alphabet (used by [[Ildarin]] and [[Anglic]].)

# Comprehensive Transcription

Of these schemes, we will focus here on the Comprehensive Transcription (CT), which is the most unambiguous transliteration method. CT is generally favoured by [[Meta|Metas]] due to its sole reliance on upper- and lowercase [[Ildarin]] letters without the use of diacritics.

## Hierograms

The Draconic language is written by means of hierograms. Each hierogram is a phonetic representation of a single syllable and can denote either a word or a grammatical prefix. Individual hierograms are generally combined to form longer, inflected, words.

All hierograms within a compound word, including all grammatical prefixes, are connected by a horizontal line at the top of the hierogram. Most words consist of at least two hierograms, a simple grammatical prefix, followed by the proper word.

An individual hierogram contains either one (in the case of a grammatical prefix) or two (in the case of all proper words) "central" consonants, a vowel and optionally a "modifying" consonant for each of the central consonants. 

The construction of a hierogram is illustrated by means of the syllable *KAnTHr* (the root of the word meaning 'amount' or 'number').

Some grammatical prefixes may contain only a vowel, in which case a _null_ consonant is used, which is identical to the symbol for zero.

## Central Consonants

In CT, the central consonants are always capitalized, to distinguish them from the anterior and posterior modifying consonants. The draconic language has 10 central consonants:

|--|--|

|Z | as in 'zap'|
|D | as in 'door'|

|N | as in 'not'|

|G | as in 'gate'|
|S | as in 'silver'|

## Vowels 
Draconic uses 8 vowels, each of which posses a stressed and an unstressed form. In CT, the unstressed vowels are written in lowercase, and the stressed forms are written in uppercase. 

|  | |  |  |
|-|-|-|-|
| a  | as in 'pass'.         | A  | as the 'aa' in Eldmeric 'aap'. |
| ae | as the 'y' in 'by'.   | AE | as the 'ij' in Eldmeric 'bij'. |
| au | as 'ow' in 'how'.     | AU | as 'au' but longer and more "rounded".|
| e  | as in 'bent'          | E  | as the 'ay' in 'bay'|
| i  | as in 'if'            | I  | as 'i' but longer, sometimes resembling an "iyi" sound.|
| o  | as in 'morning'       | O  | as the 'oa' in 'boat'. |
| u  | as the 'oo' 'boot'    | U  | as 'u' but longer. |
| y  | as the 'ee' in 'seek'.| Y  | as 'y' but longer. |

In written draconic, unstressed vowels, that are not part of a prefix carrying connotation are often omitted. Generally, when transcribing dranonic, they are re-inserted for clarity.

## Modifying Consonants

The central consonants are often modified through a modifying consonant. In proper draconic, these modifying consonants are not considered separate from the central consonant, but are said to "shape" the central consonant. 

Linguists make a distinction between anterior and posterior modifying consonants, where the anterior consonant is sounded before the central consonant, and the posterior is sounded after. Modifying consonants can be sometimes combined, but only in anterior-posterior pairs. 

In CT, the four anterior consonants are rendered as:

|  | Pronunciation|
|-|-|
|l\*|somewhere between the l in 'like' and the y in 'yet'.|
|s\*|somewhere between the s in 'silver' and the sh in 'shine'.|
|n\*|as in 'not'. If an anterior 'n' is applied to a central 'N', then the combined 'nN' is pronounced like 'm' in 'more'.|
|k\*|as in 'kiln'|

Likewise, the three posterior consonants are rendered as:

|  | Pronunciation|
|-|-|
|\*h| gives the central consonant and subsequent vowel a “guttural” modification|
|\*r| a rolling 'r' as in the Eldmeric 'raam'.
|\*j| as the 'y' in 'yet'.|

These can be combined into a total of 19 possibly modifying consonants:

| |
|-|-|-|-|
| - |  \*h |  \*r |  \*j |
|l\*| l\*h | l\*r | l\*j |
|s\*| s\*h | s\*r | s\*j |
|n\*| n\*h | n\*r | n\*j |
|k\*| k\*h | k\*r | k\*j |

## Disallowed Combination

Some combinations of central and modifying consonants are never used:
- 's\*' is never combined with 'S' or 'Z'.
- 'k\*' is never combined with 'K'.
- '\*r' is never combined with 'R'.
- 'Nr' is preferred over 'nR', unless followed by a posterior consonant (e.g. nRj).
- 'Kr' is preferred over 'kR',  unless followed by a posterior consonant (e.g. kRj).
- 'sR' is preferred of 'Sr', unless preceded by an anterior consonant (e.g. kSr). 

# Standard Transcription
While unambiguous, the Comprehensive Transcription (CT) can be cumbersome and hard to read. As such, [[Draconic]] is more frequently transcribed using more legible, generally lowercase, notation also known as Standard Transcription.

In practice, there is not one, but many regional and cultural variants of ST, however, common transcriptions rules include:
- Any omitted unstressed vowels are reinserted.
- If there is a possible ambiguity on whether a consonant is central or modifying, an apostrophe is inserted between consonant groups.
- 'm' is substituted for 'nN'.
- 'x' is substituted for 'kS'.
- A double 'CH', i.e., 'CHCH' is replaced with 'cch'.
- Stressed vowels are written with an acute (on the first vowel if applicable) , e.g., 'A' becomes 'á', 'Y' becomes 'ý', 'AU' becomes 'áu'.
- 'æ' is often substituted for 'ae', 
- 'ǽ' is often substituted for "AE".  In [[Ildarin]] transliteration, 'ei' is sometimes instead.
- 'þ' is sometimes substituted for 'TH'.
- Sometimes, especially in older transliterations, the 'b' is used for 'V'.
- In [[Anglic]] speaking countries, the 'kh' is sometimes substituted for 'CH', and the 'c' is used instead of 'K' and 'k'. Furthermore, "ow" may be used instead of "au".
